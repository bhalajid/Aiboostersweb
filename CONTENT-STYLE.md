# Content style guide — AIBoosters Consulting

The site is checked against this on every build (`test/release.sh`). If you change
the vocabulary, change it here and in the test at the same time.

## Canonical vocabulary — one word per concept

| Concept | Use | Never use |
|---|---|---|
| What we do — the nine | **capabilities** | services, offerings, disciplines, service lines, pillars, practice areas |
| The three most engagements run through | **core capabilities** | pillars, disciplines, main services |
| The nine values in the mark | **principles** | values, tenets, "the nine" on its own |
| A client project | **engagement** | job, deal, project, gig |
| Our frameworks and accelerators | **products** | tools, solutions, platforms |
| The people who buy | **clients** | customers, accounts |

**Why "capabilities" and not "services".** Services reads transactional and
commoditised. Capabilities is the standard term in enterprise consulting and implies
depth the buyer is paying for. The nav label, the page title, the footer heading and
the body copy must all agree — a nav that says "Services" pointing at a page titled
"Capabilities" is exactly the kind of drift that makes a firm look unconsidered.

## The two nines — keep them distinct

There are deliberately two sets of nine, and they must never blur:

- **Nine capabilities** — what we sell.
- **Nine principles** — what the mark stands for (Trustworthy, Human, Confident,
  Elegant, Timeless, Enterprise, Premium, Innovative, Intelligent).

Always qualify which. Never write "the nine" unqualified. The principles section
should always be framed around the mark, so a reader can't mistake it for a service
list.

## Structure of the offer

> Nine capabilities. Three of them are core — Platform Engineering, AI Integration,
> Deployment & Development — because almost every engagement runs through them. The
> other six join when the work calls for them.

The three are a **subset** of the nine, not a parent category. Do not describe them
as containing the other six.

## Voice

- **British English.** The firm sells into Europe. -ise, -isation, -ised.
  (Exception: `autocomplete="organization"` is an HTML spec token — leave it.)
- **Plain, declarative, specific.** "We ship into production" beats "we deliver
  transformative outcomes."
- **No superlatives we cannot evidence.** No "world-class", "cutting-edge",
  "best-in-class", "revolutionary".
- **Say the uncomfortable thing.** The Work page states plainly that it shows
  engagement patterns rather than named clients. That candour is the differentiator —
  do not sand it off.
- **Never imply permanent dependency.** Engagements are designed to end with the
  client's team running the system.
- **Numbers must be defensible.** If a figure appears on the site, be ready to
  explain how it was derived in a briefing.

## Claims that need sign-off before they change

- Product status badges (only "AI Readiness Assessment" is Available now)
- Any named client or case study
- The automation demo timings
- Anything describing the preview assistant as live AI
